Utilities and grid planners generally select a storage technology based on the required discharge
duration, since no single technology is cost-optimal across all durations. For sub-four-hour
applications such as frequency regulation, voltage support, and smoothing short-term renewable
variability, lithium-ion batteries currently offer the best combination of cost, efficiency, and fast
response. For four-to-twelve-hour applications, such as shifting solar output from midday into the
evening demand peak, flow batteries and, where geography allows, pumped hydro become
increasingly competitive because their costs scale more favorably with duration. For storage
durations extending beyond a day, into multi-day or seasonal timeframes, only compressed air,
large thermal systems, and hydrogen currently offer plausible economics, since battery costs at
that scale become prohibitive.
